# Decisions

Accepted cross-task choices and their reasons are recorded here. [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md) defines the detailed behavior; [open-questions.md](open-questions.md) keeps both unresolved questions and their accepted answers.

## Scope and ownership

- The stages are **Prototype → MVP → Release**. Prototype proves the complete support conversation through an ordinary Telegram bot; MVP adds Telegram Business, Shell, and manager onboarding; Release adds automatic group creation. This order keeps account authorization and provisioning out of the initial message-routing work.
- A product exists independently of this backend. The backend stores its reference and Telegram integration but does not create or own the product. Which Shell product or remote ID is authoritative remains open for MVP.
- Prototype supports multiple products. Each has one active private forum supergroup and one dedicated support bot. The bot is reserved for this service, so preserving another webhook or backend is not required. Dedicated bots keep product routing explicit.
- One common service bot is an administrator in every product group. It creates and manages customer topics and receives manager messages; it is separate from customer-facing support bots. The group owner account, a Business account, and manager accounts are separate identities, and the Business account may differ from the group owner.
- Prototype groups and manager membership are prepared manually. A protected admin API registers integrations and reports setup readiness without Shell. This keeps the Prototype focused on message routing; CSV onboarding, invitation links, and Shell configuration belong to MVP.
- Prototype uses separate test and demo support bots, service bots, groups, webhooks, and D1 databases. Local development uses test resources; production resources are deferred. Tokens and resource IDs are assigned at deployment so testing cannot disturb the demonstration setup.
- The Prototype prioritizes serverless operation and free tiers: TypeScript, Hono, grammY, Zod, Cloudflare Workers Free, D1 Free, Wrangler, Vitest, with Node.js and pnpm for local tooling. This keeps webhook processing and persistence in one small deployment model.

## Conversations and delivery

- A conversation is keyed by product, customer channel, and external customer chat. It keeps one current customer topic: a closed topic is reopened, and a deleted topic is replaced with an updated mapping. This preserves a continuing conversation without mixing products or channels.
- A topic uses the customer's current Telegram display name or `Client <chat ID>` when no name is available. Later name changes update its title, not its conversation identity.
- Any ordinary message from a non-bot member allowed to write in a mapped customer topic is an external reply in Prototype, MVP, and Release. A CSV manager record is not an extra sending permission: Telegram group access determines who can write. In Prototype the customer's original support bot delivers the reply; internal discussion stays outside mapped customer topics.
- Prototype relays text, photos up to 10 MB, and documents and voice messages up to 20 MB. Media is downloaded through the receiving bot and uploaded through the sending bot because their `file_id` values cannot be shared. Supported forwarded content and Telegram replies become ordinary new messages without forwarding metadata or a link to the original message. Reply-to linkage is not planned for Prototype, MVP, or Release.
- An unsupported or oversized first customer message still creates a topic. The topic shows why the content was not delivered, and the sender receives a notice. Confirmed outbound failures also appear in the topic so neither side mistakes a failed transfer for success.
- Webhook updates are deduplicated while their IDs are stored for seven days; delivery statuses are kept for 30 days, and conversation mappings until integration deletion. These periods bound deduplication data and delivery history while keeping conversation routing available.
- A manager delivery attempt is stored before calling Telegram. If its result is unknown, the topic warns that the customer may already have received it and the backend does not retry automatically. A manager may explicitly send another message as a new attempt. This avoids an automatic duplicate reply.
- Topic creation is reserved per conversation before calling Telegram. An unknown creation result is not retried automatically: the customer is told that transfer to support is unconfirmed, and a separate protected operational API shows the unresolved conversation without message contents or secrets. After checking Telegram, an operator links the existing topic or authorizes another attempt. This avoids a second topic and keeps operational review separate from the setup-readiness API.

## MVP

- A product needs at least one customer channel: an ordinary support bot, Telegram Business, or both. When both channels are configured, their conversation keys and topics stay separate; the same topic-recovery and seven-day deduplication rules apply to each. One shared platform Business connector bot handles Business connections; it is distinct from the service bot in groups.
- A Business connection is paired to one product. An account already connected to another Business bot must change that connection in Telegram before it can be connected here. The owner chooses accessible chats in Telegram; Telegram enforces that scope, while the backend handles delivered Business messages without keeping another allowed-chat list. Manual replies from the Business account appear in the topic as already sent and are not delivered twice. Manager replies are sent on behalf of that account when Telegram permits; required rights and loss-of-permission behavior remain open.
- Shell links an existing group and displays backend-checked status for each configured channel and the group; it does not create groups in MVP or receive stored Telegram credentials after registration. A Business-only product does not require an ordinary-bot readiness check. Group provisioning stays in Release.
- CSV import prepares a separate invitation link for each valid expected-manager row and reports invalid rows. An email address is not a Telegram identity; required columns and link distribution remain open.
- Invitation links require a Telegram join request. Shell shows the applicant account beside the CSV record, and the product owner confirms the person before the backend asks Telegram to approve. Telegram ID and joined status are stored only after Telegram confirms success; a failed approval remains visible without marking the manager joined. The link alone cannot prove identity, and managers do not get Telegram administrator rights by default.

## Release

- The product owner approves a QR login in an already signed-in Telegram app. A separate MTProto component creates a private forum supergroup under that authorized user account only if the product has no group. Shell and the operator do not receive an exported user session. MTProto provisioning stays outside normal message routing; session storage and QR fallback remain open.
- Before group creation, the backend stores a per-product operation and a unique marker placed in the new group's description. It records a confirmed group ID before bot setup. If creation is uncertain, the authorized account searches its groups for that marker, reauthorizing by QR if necessary. An unresolved result requires review rather than automatic creation of another group. The marker is removed after successful setup.
- Provisioning adds the common service bot and verifies the rights required for topics, messages, and MVP join requests before reporting readiness. A retry continues configuring a recorded group. Manager onboarding continues through MVP invitation links; direct MTProto invitations are outside the planned Release.

When an accepted choice changes, update this file and the affected specification and issues. Unresolved details remain in [open-questions.md](open-questions.md).
